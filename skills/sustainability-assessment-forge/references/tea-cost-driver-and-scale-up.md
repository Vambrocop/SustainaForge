# TEA Cost Driver and Scale-Up

Use this reference when a paper claims low cost, competitiveness, or commercial promise.

Check:

- production capacity;
- annual operating hours and utilization;
- plant lifetime;
- discount rate or financing basis;
- CAPEX source and scaling rule;
- OPEX breakdown;
- feedstock and electricity price assumptions;
- labor, maintenance, overhead, and replacement assumptions;
- product price basis or minimum selling price calculation;
- coproduct revenue treatment.

Ask whether the cost structure is:

- bottom-up and process-based;
- benchmarked to an existing commercial route;
- a rough back-of-the-envelope estimate;
- dependent on one heroic assumption such as free feedstock or ultra-cheap power.

Scale-up realism:

- Is productivity demonstrated for long enough to matter?
- Are durability and replacement intervals modeled?
- Are separations, recycle loops, and balance-of-plant included?
- Are material compatibility, corrosion, membrane life, or catalyst replacement visible?
- Is the claimed scale consistent with the experimental basis?

Interpretation rule:

A TEA can be useful even when uncertain, but only if the paper shows which assumptions dominate the result and does not hide them behind one headline cost number.
