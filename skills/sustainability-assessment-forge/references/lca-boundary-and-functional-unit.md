# LCA Boundary and Functional Unit

Use this reference when a paper makes environmental claims but the comparison basis may not be stable.

Ask:

- What is the actual service being compared?
- Is the functional unit a product mass, an energy service, a treatment service, a carbon-removal service, or something else?
- Is the reference flow stated clearly enough to reconstruct the calculation?
- Are the boundaries cradle-to-gate, gate-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, or partially truncated?
- Are transport, separations, auxiliaries, infrastructure, and end-of-life included or omitted?
- Does coproduct handling use allocation, system expansion, or substitution?
- Would a different electricity mix or utilization pattern reverse the result?

Common failure modes:

- comparing yield-based claims with service-based claims;
- using product mass where delivered function should be used;
- omitting energy-intensive purification or compression;
- excluding short-lived consumables or catalysts from a supposedly full-system comparison;
- hiding burden shifting outside the stated boundary.

Interpretation rule:

If the functional unit is unfair or the boundary is selective, the LCA may still be useful as a partial engineering analysis, but it is not a full comparative sustainability verdict.
